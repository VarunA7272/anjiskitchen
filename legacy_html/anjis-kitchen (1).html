Our Story

It began on a two-burner stove, not a business plan.

Anji's Kitchen started the way most real Indian food does — with one woman, one steel dabba, and a habit of cooking a little extra "just in case someone drops by." The someones kept dropping by. Then they started asking for tiffins. Then Jabalpur started asking.

Nothing here is built for a menu photoshoot. The dhokla is steamed the way it was steamed for birthday mornings. The shrikhand is hung and strained overnight, not rushed. We simply decided to let more people sit at the same table.

— Anjali, Anji's Kitchen
The Morning Kitchen

Before the first order, the stove is already lit.

Every batch begins the same unhurried way — chopped by hand, tempered on real flame, plated the way it would be at home.

Po

Poha

Flattened rice, hand-washed thrice, tempered with mustard seed, curry leaf and a squeeze of fresh lemon.

Upma

Dh

Dhokla

Steamed till impossibly soft, tempered on top, never microwaved-soft — always steam-soft.

Sw

Sandwiches

Stacked and pressed to order, so the first bite still has that just-off-the-tawa warmth.

Ch

Ground fresh each morning in small batches — mint, coconut, garlic — never bottled, never stored.
